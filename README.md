# HRMS

Human Resource Management System, rebuilt step by step with a simple, conventional stack.
The database schema is added **one model at a time**; every feature is built on top of it.

## Stack

| Part | Technology |
|---|---|
| Backend | Node.js 22, Express 5, TypeScript 7 |
| API layer | oRPC 1.15 - typed procedures; the frontend gets backend types automatically |
| Database | PostgreSQL + Prisma 7 (schema, migrations, typed client) |
| Validation | zod 4 |
| Excel | exceljs 4.4 (employee import template + import, directory export) |
| Letters / e-mail | TipTap 3 (letter template editor), pdfmake 0.3 (offer letter PDFs), pdf-lib 1.17 (HR's paper forms printed filled in), Fontsource (Roboto - the PDF's default font, so the editor's pages break where the PDF's do - and the WordCraft open fonts for letters: Carlito, Caladea, Arimo, Tinos, Cousine, Inter, Source Sans 3, Source Serif 4, JetBrains Mono), nodemailer 10 (offer e-mails, one-time codes) |
| Frontend | React 19, Vite 8, TypeScript 7 |
| UI | Tailwind CSS 4 + shadcn/ui (Base UI) + lucide icons |
| Data fetching | TanStack Query 5 (through oRPC's query helpers) |
| Routing | React Router 8 |

Types flow in one direction, with nothing written twice:
Prisma schema -> Prisma client -> backend procedures -> frontend (`orpc.*`).
Rename a field in the schema and every place that uses it shows a TypeScript error.

## Two portals, home and attendance reports (2026-11-24, portals 2026-11-25)

Everyone signs in to **Employee self-service** (`/ess/...`): one's own attendance, leave, pay & career, profile and
requests, plus one's team and HOD department (team attendance and leave, the steps named to them on Approvals). Picking a
role in the account menu opens the **role portal** (`/manage/...`): the role's pages, its approval steps and the requests
raised for others; its employee lists show the role's records plus one's own team. Payroll runs require explicit
whole-company Payroll access and have no team/HOD fallback. One's own actions (leave, comp-off,
regularizations, shift changes, own documents) are refused there. Each browser tab keeps its own choice; a page of the
other portal shows a switch screen and comes back to it, and the old addresses redirect.

The self-service home (`/ess`, redesigned 2026-11-27 after Crextio's composition - `crextio-ref/NOTES.md`) opens with the
greeting, the attendance month as a segmented strip and big headline numbers (a team: its size, punched in today, decisions
waiting; otherwise: leave days left, days to fix, days present). Below: "Today" as a ring (the shift so far by the clock,
the first punch in the middle, last out / worked / late beside it), "My week" as bars of hours worked, "Needs your
attention" (the days of the last 30 still to fix, each opening its regularization at `/ess/attendance?regularize=<date>`),
leave, coming holidays, recent requests and details - for a manager also the decisions named to them, People today (in /
total, refreshing every 2 minutes) and who is away this week. **What needs the employee comes first** (the user,
2026-11-27): the home is one regular grid of tiles of one height, one or two columns wide, four to a row; decisions
waiting (past due first) and days to fix are the first tiles; what needs nothing (no decisions, nobody away, no requests,
every day in order) gets no tile, only a check in the calm line under the grid. **Self-service covers oneself and one's direct reports only** (the user,
2026-11-27): the home's team cards and numbers use the `DIRECT` scope (`attendance.overview` `directReports`); the whole
reporting tree and the HOD department stay on Team attendance / Team leave and in the role portal. The role home (`/manage`) shows the role's decisions, open joining tasks and accepted offers (Onboarding), and the
role's numbers, each only with the access its reader needs (2026-11-27): the attendance month to close (Attendance setup
Edit for every company), payroll runs (Payroll View, no amounts), joiners & leavers (Employees View) and who is away
(Leave View); then People today in its access, the requests raised for others and the role's pages. Since 2026-11-27 it
has the same headline numbers as `/ess`, from the role's access. Who is in today is read once, on the attendance
cycle under that header (Attendance setup's start day; on leave includes on duty, and absent stays separate). Below it,
one regular tile grid, most urgent first: decisions waiting, a month over and not locked, open joining tasks, a pay
month without a run; what needs nothing gets a check in the calm line; the role's pages complete the last row. Motion on the homes
only tells something (a changed number glows once, bars grow to their value, the "now" marker slides) and stops under
reduced motion. The cream/charcoal/gold tiles follow Crextio's composition, in one grid of equal height.
Business priorities come from main. Both reuse existing typed APIs, without schema changes, role-name
checks or invented payroll/KPI values. Switching reloads once at the new portal.

`/attendance-reports` shows attendance detail and exceptions over explicit calendar work dates. People scope, dates,
search, company/plant/department/status and exception filters persist in the URL. Summaries cover every matching page;
Excel exports all matching stored rows (maximum 20,000, refusing larger files). Whole-company/group viewing and exports
follow Attendance View data access. Raw-punch drill-down/export reuses night-shift ownership and shows only retained
source metadata. Missing days are not inferred as absence; current placement/team membership is used.

## Employee submissions (2026-11-25)

Employees can add a PDF/JPG/PNG up to 5 MB in **My profile → Documents**. Submissions remain pending until another
scoped HR reviewer verifies or rejects them. Employees see review status/reasons and can submit a corrected copy.
An existing verified copy stays current while its replacement waits; pending employee evidence does not satisfy
consent checks. HR reviews through the employee's Documents tab; employees cannot verify/delete/restore submissions.
Family/nominee requests, scheduled report emails, additional imports and biometric stages remain in BACKLOG item 32.

## Recorded joiners and leavers (2026-11-25)

**People → Joiners & leavers** (`/employees?view=movements`) requires Employee directory & job View. Inclusive dates,
joining/leaving type, name/code and company/plant/department filters stay in the URL. Company-wide and group-wide views
follow the active role's permitted range; totals and full Excel exports share those filters and scope. One employee
may have both events. Viewing/exporting above 20,000 matching events is refused rather than truncated.

This uses recorded employee dates and current placement/status, including imported/directly added employees. Future
dates are marked. Leaving dates do not confirm approved exits, clearance or settlement; missing rehire/exit history is
not reconstructed. No salary, identity, bank or medical data is selected. The register itself requires no schema change;
the separate daily email configuration below uses the approved setting model. Scheduled delivery remains deferred.

Daily movement email settings (2026-11-25): only the **active Super Admin** sees **Email settings** in People → Joiners
& leavers. Choose the HR Head role (must have Employee directory & job View); defaults are 09:00 IST, previous-day
coverage and summaries even on no-movement days. Settings are global, audited and reject stale edits. Configuration
is saved with delivery disabled: scheduled email, extra recipients and safe delivery/retries remain the next stage.
The approved migration adds only DailyMovementReportSetting; apply pending migrations to the app database before use.

## Getting started

Prerequisites: Node.js 22.12+ and PostgreSQL running locally.

```bash
# 1. Install everything (both apps) from the root
npm install

# 2. Create backend/.env from the example, set your DB password and generate DATA_ENCRYPTION_KEY
#    (command is in the file). Back the key up - without it encrypted Aadhaar/PAN/bank numbers are unreadable.
#    Uploaded documents go to backend/storage/ (STORAGE_DIR) - back that folder up with the database.
#    E-mail: set SMTP_* and MAIL_FROM to send offer e-mails; without SMTP_HOST they are saved as .eml files in
#    backend/storage/outbox/. APP_URL is the web address candidates open their offer link on.
cp backend/.env.example backend/.env

# 3. Create the database (once)
psql -U postgres -c "CREATE DATABASE hrms_new;"

# 4. Apply migrations (once there are models)
npm run db:migrate

# 5. Start backend + frontend together
npm run dev
```

- Tests: set `TEST_DATABASE_URL` in `backend/.env` (a database whose name ends in `_test`, see `.env.example`), then
  `npm run test`. The tests migrate it themselves and add their own uniquely named data - nothing is deleted.

- Frontend: http://localhost:5180 - log in with an employee code; on an empty database create the first super admin with `npm run create-admin` (every login signs in to Employee self-service; the Super Admin role is picked in the account menu - 2026-11-25), later logins with `npm run set-password` or the Roles & access page.
  For testing, list accounts in `frontend/.env.development.local` (`VITE_TEST_ACCOUNTS`, see `frontend/.env.example`; never
  committed): the login page then shows them for one-click sign-in (development only, never in a production build).
- API: http://localhost:5200/api/rpc (health check: `POST /api/rpc/health/check`)

In development the frontend calls `/api/...` and Vite forwards it to the backend, so there are no CORS issues.

## API

**Every API is documented in [API_TRACKER.md](API_TRACKER.md)**: path, who can access it, input fields,
what it returns, what it does and its errors, plus the total count. Search it before adding an API so nothing
is built twice, and update it in the same change whenever an API changes (`npm run check:api-tracker` verifies).

## Adding a table

1. Add the model to the right domain file in `backend/prisma/schema/` (or create a new one, e.g.
   `organization.prisma`). Follow the conventions in `_base.prisma`.
2. `npm run db:migrate -- --name add_departments` - creates the SQL migration, applies it, and regenerates the
   typed client.
3. Commit the schema change **and** the new folder in `prisma/migrations/` together.

## Adding a feature

**Backend** - every request goes through three layers: `router -> service -> repository -> database`.
Create `backend/src/modules/<feature>/`:

| File | Layer | Responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| `<feature>.router.ts` | routes + controller | auth, input schema -> call one service function -> return. No logic. |
| `<feature>.service.ts` | business | rules and decisions (e.g. throw `NOT_FOUND`), transactions; calls repositories |
| `<feature>.repository.ts` | data access | the only place with Prisma queries; explicit `select`, never whole rows |
| `<feature>.schema.ts` | - | zod schemas for procedure inputs |
| `index.ts` | - | public API: the router + the service namespace (+ the schema namespace when other modules validate the same data); the repository stays private |

There is no separate controller layer - the oRPC procedure already is the controller.

```ts
// employees.repository.ts - data access
export function findByCode(employeeCode: string, db: DbClient = prisma) {
  return db.employee.findUnique({ where: { employeeCode }, select: employeeSelect })
}

// employees.service.ts - business rules
export async function getByCode(employeeCode: string, db?: DbClient) {
  const employee = await employeesRepository.findByCode(employeeCode, db)
  if (!employee) throw new ORPCError('NOT_FOUND', { message: `No employee found with code ${employeeCode}` })
  return employee
}

// departments.router.ts - routes + controller: who may call it (area + level), input, one service call
export const departmentsRouter = {
  list: authedProcedure.input(listInput).handler(({ input }) => departmentsService.listDepartments(input?.includeInactive)),
  create: accessProcedure('ORGANIZATION', 'WRITE')
    .input(createDepartmentInput)
    .handler(({ input }) => departmentsService.createDepartment(input)),
}
```

Then add the router to `backend/src/router.ts` (`employees: employeesRouter`).

**Access:** reading APIs use `accessProcedure(area, 'READ')`, changing APIs `accessProcedure(area, 'WRITE')`.
Roles are data (the "Roles & access" page), so code never names a role. Someone with several roles works in one at a
time: they pick it in the account menu (per tab), and only that role's areas and data access count. A new module adds its area to
`AccessArea` in `access.prisma` and its View / Edit text to `AREA_INFO` in `modules/accounts/roles/roles.service.ts`;
the frontend shows pages and buttons with `useAccess().can(area, level)`.

**Where every kind of file goes, naming, and the dependency rules are defined in [CLAUDE.md](CLAUDE.md)
(section 1, "Folder structure").** They apply to everyone - people and AI assistants. Follow them for every change;
the structure itself only changes by agreement, and then CLAUDE.md is updated in the same change.
Codex and other AI agents start from [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md), which sends them to the same CLAUDE.md.

Errors: throw `new ORPCError('NOT_FOUND', { message: 'Department not found' })` for expected failures.
Invalid input (400 with a list of field issues) and common Prisma errors (duplicate -> 409, missing -> 404) are
converted automatically in `core/rpc/errors.ts`.

**Frontend** - no API types to write; they come from the backend:

```tsx
const departments = useQuery(orpc.departments.list.queryOptions())
const create = useMutation(orpc.departments.create.mutationOptions())
```

Dates and files work as-is: a `Date` returned by the backend arrives in the browser as a `Date`.

## Payroll — Stages 1, 2 and the days of stage 3 (2026-11-27)

`/manage/payroll` is in the role portal under Payroll. Give the role the dedicated **Payroll** View/Edit area and
data access covering a whole company (every plant/department). Salary-profile access does not imply Payroll access.
Partial rows never combine into a whole-company grant; group-wide access can cover all companies. Existing roles
receive no automatic grant; the built-in Super Admin includes the new area.

Create/view regular monthly India payroll **drafts**, one per company/month. The company picker offers active India
companies within full-company access. The attendance window comes from attendance settings (e.g. November = 26 October
to 25 November) and is saved with the run. If the preview changes before creation, reopen the form and review it.
Later setting changes do not rewrite saved dates. Retired companies' existing runs remain accessible within scope.
**The saved window is the run's pay period** (2026-11-27): with the start day of 26 the March run pays 26 February to
25 March. The start day is changed in Attendance setup (Month lock), not in code; runs created afterwards follow it.

**Stage 2:** creating a draft also copies its people - everyone the job timeline places in the company on at least one
day of the pay period (consultants included) - with the salary and placement in force on each day; an increment or
transfer inside the period makes two stretches. Open a run (`/manage/payroll/:id`) for its **People** (days, stretches, days without a
salary) and **Inputs** (amounts for this month only, e.g. an incentive or a canteen recovery, with a reason). "Refresh
from employee records" rebuilds the copy while the run is a draft; people with inputs are never dropped. Only someone
with Payroll Edit over the whole company enters inputs, never for themselves. Salary figures also need **Salary** View
over the whole company. Items typed each month are salary components marked "Entered each month in payroll" on
Organization › Salary components (they never appear in a salary breakup); components also get a short code there.

**Stage 3, the days and the earnings (2026-11-27):** once the attendance month of the pay period is locked (Attendance
setup › Month lock), **Work out pay** on the run page copies each person's days into the run: every half is paid
(present, on duty, weekly off, holiday, paid leave), unpaid (absent, missed punch, leave without pay - and a half the
late rule made absent, unless regularized) or "to check" (attendance has no day, or a leave half with no approved
leave). From those days it works out what each person earned: a day is worth the monthly amount ÷ the days of the pay
period, each line rounded to the nearest rupee; an earning is cut for unpaid days unless its salary component is set
to "Paid in full" (Organization › Salary components); inputs are paid as typed. Someone whose pay cannot be worked out
(no salary on some days, days missing in attendance, another currency) is held and listed under "Pay not worked out" -
never guessed. Changing a person's inputs afterwards marks them "Work out again". The people table shows days in the
run, paid, unpaid and earned; a person's view shows each day and each earned line. Earned amounts are salary figures
(Salary View over the whole company). The run keeps this copy even if attendance is opened again later, and says so
when it was. Refreshing the people removes the days and the pay - work it out again. People absent on every working
day with no punch at all are listed
under "To check" (most likely they do not punch - mark them **Not tracked** on Attendance setup › Schedules while
the month is open: from that day they count present on their working days, whatever the punches say; leave, off days and
HR's corrections stay. Needs Attendance setup: Edit covering the employee; migration `attendance_not_tracked`).

**Stage 4, deductions and net pay (2026-11-27):** **Payroll › Statutory settings** (`/manage/payroll/statutory`) holds
every rate, ceiling and slab as a dated row with its notification - PF, EPS, EDLI and ESIC rates and ceilings, the
professional-tax slabs and labour welfare fund of each State, the new-regime income-tax slabs, standard deduction,
rebate and cess - plus each employee's **opening tax figures** (earned and deducted earlier in the tax year outside this
system). Nothing is filled in for you: enter them from the official notifications (Payroll Edit for every company) and
have the CA check them. Then **Work out pay** also works out PF (on the salary components ticked "PF wages", up to the
ceiling - or on full wages where the employee's PF details say so - for employees whose statutory details say "PF
applicable"), ESIC (when the salary at the start of the ESIC period was within the ceiling), professional tax and LWF
(by the State of the work location), the month's TDS (new regime; HR types it for the old regime or a consultant) and
the typed deductions, and shows the net. Anyone whose pay cannot be worked out - no work location, a rate not in
force, a statutory component not set, deductions over half of the wages - is held with the reason (what they earned
still shows; deductions and net wait). Mark the components
on Organization › Salary components (PF wages, ESI wages, taxable; the statutory amount a deduction carries) and the PF
facts and tax regime on the employee's "PF, ESIC and payroll" details.

**Stage 5, approval and finalising (2026-11-27):** the run's **Checks** tab lists warnings about its people (no salary
account, no PAN, a big change from last month, a PF member without UAN, leaving, a duplicate input...); each must be
acknowledged with a comment (several at once). **Send for approval** works the pay out once more and sends the run along
the **Pay run** approval chain - set it up first on Approvals › Approval rules with roles only, each having Payroll View
and Salary View over the whole company (e.g. Payroll head, then Finance head). Sending is refused while anyone's pay is
held or a warning is open. While it waits nothing in the run changes; the sender never approves it; it can be withdrawn.
On the last approval the run is **Final** and never changes (a correction comes later as arrears); rejected or withdrawn,
it is a draft again. **Run settings** on Statutory settings hold the tolerances: whether earlier months' TDS counts draft
runs (default: finalised only), the big-change % (20) and the EPS warning months (2).

**Stage 6, salary register and payslips (2026-11-27):** **Salary register** on a run downloads Excel with every person's
days and amounts and the totals per component (before the run is final it is marked DRAFT). Set up a payslip layout first
on **Letter templates › Payslip** - a new one starts like HR's greytHR payslip (details, earnings and deductions, net pay in
words, leave); move, add or remove anything, and add a header image if wanted. On a final run, **Release payslips** with a
template (open a person and **Preview payslip** to check first): the worker makes each payslip within minutes, keeps it
(it never changes) and e-mails the employee a link; employees open theirs in **Pay & career › Payslips**. Payslips show the
full bank account number and PAN, so they are never attached to e-mails. The background worker must be running (`npm run
worker -w backend`; `npm run dev` starts it).

**Stage 6, salary payments (2026-11-28):** give the new **Payroll payments** area (Roles & access) to whoever pays - finance,
HR or both; it is apart from Payroll. **Salary payments** (`/manage/salary-payments`) lists everyone of the approved runs
not paid yet (to pay, no salary account, returned, held, in a batch) with the days overdue; pick people of one company and
**Make a batch** (bank transfer, cheques or cash). In the batch: download the plain **Payment list** (full account numbers -
until finance gives the bank's own format), pay it at the bank, **Mark sent**, then **Mark paid** with the day and the bank's
reference (each cheque with its number); a payment the bank sends back is **Returned** with its reason and paid again in a
later batch. Pay can be held with a reason and released. "Wages due" on Run settings (default 7 days after the pay
period) sets the days overdue. Nobody pays themselves.

**Stage 6, statutory deposits (2026-11-28):** give the new **Statutory deposits** area (Roles & access) to whoever files PF,
ESIC, professional tax and LWF; each person's amounts also need Salary View. First enter the due dates on **Payroll ›
Statutory settings › Due dates** (PF and ESIC once, professional tax and LWF per State - nothing is filled in). **Statutory
deposits** (`/manage/statutory-deposits`) lists each company's month and scheme of the approved runs with what is due, what
is deposited and the due date - the open ones first. Open a month: **Record a return / challan** (its reference, the day it
was filed, the contributions, any admin charges or interest), then **Paid** with the day and reference; download
**Figures** (each person's PF, ESIC, PT and LWF - a plain sheet, not the upload file). For PF and ESIC, mark anyone who
could not go into the return as **left out** (no UAN, details not matching...) and later **taken in** by the supplementary
return. The people who record deposits get a daily e-mail from 3 days (Run settings) before a due date until the challan is
paid; employees see their own months in **Pay & career › Statutory deposits**.

The earnings rule is checked against a greytHR salary register in the parallel run (BACKLOG item 31). The bank file and
statutory files follow the staged plan in BACKLOG.md, with each new model/field agreed before
migration. Apply the migrations with `npm run db:migrate` (stage 2: `payroll_stage2_people_inputs`; stage 3:
`payroll_stage3_days`, `payroll_stage3_amounts`; stage 4: `payroll_stage4_deductions`; stage 5: `payroll_stage5_approval`;
stage 6: `payroll_stage6_payslips`, `payroll_stage6_payments`, `payroll_stage6_deposits`).
